# Multiplayer Transfer Market Overhaul — Full Implementation Plan

## 1. Goals

Replace the current fragmented transfer logic with a multiplayer-safe market where:

* Human and AI clubs use the same market infrastructure.
* Auctions are the only way a contracted player is permanently transferred between clubs.
* Free agents have their own competitive signing market.
* Loans remain a separate first-come, first-served market with no loan fee.
* Sellers cannot select buyers.
* Clubs cannot privately transfer arbitrary amounts of money to one another.
* Disposable/multi-account teams cannot easily funnel players or money into another team.
* AI clubs make rational squad-management decisions and never knowingly commit to transactions that make them financially insolvent.
* AI never has access to hidden bid information or hidden player-development information.
* Board confidence and manager-sacking mechanics are removed.
* The concepts of star players and club reputation are removed from transfer-market behavior and, where they still exist elsewhere, removed from the game entirely.
* Existing `player.value` remains the single reference valuation used by the market.

# 2. Final Market Structure

Keep the existing transfer-page separation:

```text
┌────────────┬─────────────┬─────────┬───────────────┐
│ Auctions   │ Free Agents │ Loans   │ List for Sale │
└────────────┴─────────────┴─────────┴───────────────┘
```

These are deliberately different markets.

### Auctions

```text
Contracted player
→ owner lists player
→ public proxy-bid auction
→ human + AI clubs compete
→ winner pays selling club
```

### Free Agents

```text
Unowned player
→ system lists player
→ public proxy-bid signing process
→ human + AI clubs compete
→ winner pays system
```

### Loans

```text
Contracted player
→ owner lists player for loan
→ public exposure period
→ listing becomes claimable
→ first eligible club to claim gets player
→ no loan fee
```

There must be no alternative player-transfer route.

# 9. Auctions — Listing Rules

Only the owning club can list a contracted player.

The seller chooses the **opening asking price** within a permitted range derived
from the player's opening-price base value (§64.1). The base is the player's own
last permanent trade price (blended linearly toward `player.value` over
`RECENT_TRADE_FADE_OVER_GAMES` games played) when one exists, otherwise
`player.value`.

Initial range configuration:

```text
MIN_AUCTION_OPENING_VALUE_RATIO = 0.60
MAX_AUCTION_OPENING_VALUE_RATIO = 1.00
```

Example (no recent trade):

```text
Player value:    $10.0M
Base:            $10.0M
Allowed opening: $6.0M – $10.0M
```

The owner therefore makes two decisions:

> Am I willing to sell this player at my chosen opening price?
> Is that opening price within the permitted range of the base value?

If not, do not list him.

The opening price is only the opening bid; competitive bidding may drive the
actual sale above it up to the bidder-specific maximum-bid cap (§10/§64.3).

# 10. Auction Maximum Price — Asymmetric Division-Gap Cap

Club-to-club transfers need a market-integrity ceiling, but a universal `150%` cap is unnecessarily restrictive when a stronger/higher-division club is buying from a weaker/lower-division club. That direction of cash flow is much less useful for multi-account funneling than a weaker club overpaying a stronger club.

The cap must therefore be **bidder-specific** and derived from the **division gap** between buyer and seller, independent of the budget curve. Division 1 is the strongest; higher numbers are weaker.

Let:

```text
Dbuyer  = buyer's division number
Dseller = seller's division number
Dmax    = total number of divisions
```

Calculate the favorable division gap:

```text
favorableGap = max(0, Dseller - Dbuyer)
```

Normalize across the current pyramid depth:

```text
normalizedGap = favorableGap / max(1, Dmax - 1)
```

Use:

```text
BASE_CLUB_TRANSFER_CAP = 1.50
MAX_CLUB_TRANSFER_CAP_BONUS = 1.50
CLUB_TRANSFER_CAP_CURVE_K = 2.00
```

Then:

```ts
curve =
    (1 - Math.exp(-CLUB_TRANSFER_CAP_CURVE_K * normalizedGap))
    / (1 - Math.exp(-CLUB_TRANSFER_CAP_CURVE_K));

capMultiplier =
    BASE_CLUB_TRANSFER_CAP
    + MAX_CLUB_TRANSFER_CAP_BONUS * curve;

maximumAllowedBid = Math.min(
    player.value * capMultiplier,
    SafeMarketBudget
);
```

Therefore:

* same-division transfer: `capMultiplier = 1.50`;
* a lower-division club buying from the same or a stronger division: `capMultiplier = 1.50`;
* a stronger-division club buying from a weaker division: the cap relaxes progressively;
* the largest possible division gap reaches exactly `3.00× player.value`;
* diminishing returns make the first few division differences matter more than later ones;
* the result adapts automatically if the number of divisions changes;
* division numbers are used directly rather than configured budgets, keeping the transfer-cap system independent from the separate budget curve.

Illustrative values with the initial parameters (relative pyramid gap → approx. cap):

```text
0%                          150%
10%                         181%
20%                         207%
30%                         229%
40%                         247%
50%                         262%
60%                         274%
70%                         284%
80%                         291%
90%                         296%
100%                        300%
```

Use the clubs' **division numbers** (from season membership tier), not current club cash/budget balances, so clubs cannot manipulate the cap by temporarily spending or hoarding money, and the budget curve never implicitly re-tunes the transfer cap.

The seller still does not choose the buyer, proxy clearing still determines the actual sale price, and every bid remains subject to `SafeMarketBudget`.

These parameters must live in central market configuration. Do not scatter the constants throughout the engine.

# 11. eBay-Style Proxy Bidding

Players submit a private:

```text
MAXIMUM BID
```

They do not repeatedly submit visible incremental bids.

Example:

```text
Opening price: $8.5M

Club A max:   $10.0M
Club B max:   $12.0M
Club C max:    $9.5M
```

Club B wins.

The visible/current bid should be approximately:

```text
secondHighestMaximum + bidIncrement
```

subject to the leader's own maximum.

Therefore:

```text
Current price ≈ $10.1M
Leader = Club B
```

Club B does not immediately pay $12M.

# 12. Proxy Bid Calculation

```ts
if (bidCount === 0) {

if (bidCount >= 2) {
    currentPrice = Math.min(
```

Always enforce:

```ts
currentPrice >= openingPrice;
```

# 17. Auction Duration and Season-Rollover Boundary

Replace the legacy `auctionDurationDays = 7` behavior with real-time 24-hour listings.

Initial configuration:

```text
AUCTION_DURATION = 24 hours
SOFT_CLOSE_WINDOW = 5 minutes
SOFT_CLOSE_EXTENSION = 5 minutes
MAX_SOFT_CLOSE_EXTENSION = 30 minutes
```

Use real server timestamps rather than matchday counters and store all deadlines in UTC.

A **club-to-club auction may not cross season rollover**. Define the season rollover timestamp as the hard market boundary for that season. A new auction may be created only when:

```ts
now
+ AUCTION_DURATION
+ MAX_SOFT_CLOSE_EXTENSION
<= seasonRolloverAt
```

This guarantees that even the maximum allowed sequence of soft-close extensions can settle before rollover.

Soft-close logic must also enforce:

```ts
deadline <= seasonRolloverAt;
```

If legacy/corrupt state somehow leaves a club-to-club auction active at rollover:

1. cancel it;
2. release all reservations;
3. preserve an audit/history record;
4. do not move the player or cash.

Market-specific rollover behavior:

```text
Club-to-club auction   may not cross rollover
Free-agent listing    may cross rollover
Unclaimed loan        cancel at rollover
Active loan           end and return player at rollover
```

A free-agent listing may cross because it has no seller ownership/transfer-cooldown ambiguity; its snapshotted value, salary demand, contract demand, opening price, and bid state remain fixed until settlement. The signing transaction belongs to the season in which it settles.

# 24. Central Financial Commitment Validation

Both humans and AI must use the same deterministic financial-validation service for auction bids, free-agent bids, and loan claims.

Do not validate only:

```ts
club.cash >= bid
```

Instead calculate the club's **lowest projected cash balance** across the funded forecast horizon.

For a proposed commitment with maximum fee `M`, define at each future financial checkpoint `t`:

```text
ProjectedCash(t) =
    currentCash
  - activeMarketReservations
  - M
  + safeExpectedIncome(t)
  - committedExpenses(t)
  - acquisitionPayroll(t)
```

Then:

```text
SafeMarketBudget =
    max(
        0,
        min_t(
            currentCash
          - activeMarketReservations
          + safeExpectedIncome(t)
          - committedExpenses(t)
          - acquisitionPayroll(t)
        )
        - minimumLiquidityReserve
    )
```

A proposed maximum is legal only when:

```ts
proposedMaximum <= SafeMarketBudget;
```

The minimum over time is important: a club that is solvent at season end but cannot make an earlier payroll checkpoint must still be rejected.

### Forecast horizon

Forecast through the end of the furthest season for which the club has already received/unlocked its budget allocation.

```text
Only current-season allocation available
→ forecast through current-season end

Upcoming-season allocation already unlocked
→ forecast through upcoming-season end
```

If an upcoming-season allocation becomes available before that season starts, it may count as cash immediately, but player contracts and salaries for that upcoming season do **not** start consuming payroll until the club actually enters that season. Player ageing/development may still progress according to the separate development rules.

### Income treatment

Guaranteed income counts at 100% once it is contractually/mathematically certain. Examples include an already-awarded season allocation or fixed scheduled payment.

Uncertain recurring income must use a conservative lower-tail estimate rather than its arithmetic mean. Use the 10th percentile:

```text
safeExpectedIncome = P10(future income distribution)
```

For club-specific income with limited history, shrink its estimate toward the division distribution:

```text
w = n / (n + 8)

P10_safe =
    w * P10_club
  + (1 - w) * P10_division
```

where `n` is the number of relevant historical observations. This prevents a handful of unusually strong gates from making a club look permanently richer than it is.

Do not count speculative income such as:

* possible future transfer proceeds;
* an auction the club is merely leading as seller;
* non-guaranteed prize money;
* promotion rewards not yet secured.

### Minimum liquidity reserve

Do not use a fixed dollar reserve or a flat percentage of club budget. Use a scale-independent operating reserve:

```text
minimumLiquidityReserve = max(
    oneFullPayrollCycle,
    P90(one-cycle forecast error)
)
```

Until enough history exists to estimate forecast error reliably:

```text
minimumLiquidityReserve = oneFullPayrollCycle
```

This automatically scales across divisions and prevents a high-budget club from receiving an artificially tiny buffer or a low-budget club from being locked out by an arbitrary absolute amount.

The validator must include:

* current cash, including negative cash;
* active maximum-bid reservations;
* current accrued payroll;
* future payroll already contractually committed within the horizon;
* inherited salary for an auction acquisition;
* generated salary for a free-agent acquisition;
* 100% borrower salary for a loan;
* stadium and other unavoidable committed expenses already modeled by the game.

AI contract renewals should use this same affordability boundary rather than the legacy `club.cash > demand` check, otherwise the AI could obey market solvency rules and immediately bypass them through contract renewal.

## 46.1 Salary From the Live Contract Market

Do not use a fixed `player.value × salaryRate` rule and do not simply copy the player's previous salary. Existing salaries are intentionally sticky between contracts, so free agency is a natural point to re-anchor the player to the current salary market.

For free agent `p`:

```text
Vp = p.value
Vi = comparable contracted player's value
Si = comparable contracted player's salary
```

Measure similarity in log-value space:

```text
di = ln(Vi / Vp)
```

Weight each contracted senior player using a Gaussian kernel:

```text
h = ln(2)
wi = exp(-(di²) / (2h²))
```

With `h = ln(2)`, players around half-to-double the free agent's value form the strongest comparison set while more distant players still contribute smoothly.

Calculate market salary using the weighted median in log-salary space:

```text
marketSalary = exp(
    weightedMedian(ln(Si), wi)
)
```

Use a weighted median rather than a mean so a few legacy/extreme contracts cannot distort the entire salary curve.

Measure effective sample size:

```text
Neff = (Σwi)² / Σ(wi²)
```

If:

```text
Neff < 25
```

gradually widen `h` until the sample is sufficiently informative.

Add small deterministic negotiation variation:

```text
salaryNoise = exp(0.05 × Z)
```

where `Z` is a deterministic standard-normal draw seeded from:

```text
playerId + freeAgentListingVersion
```

and clamped to approximately:

```text
-1.5 <= Z <= +1.5
```

This gives roughly ±8% maximum player-specific negotiation variation while remaining deterministic across restart/retry.

Then:

```text
demandedSalary = marketSalary × salaryNoise
```

Clamp the result to the weighted 5th-95th salary-percentile band of the same comparable population and round it using the game's normal salary-money rounding helper. The percentile clamp scales automatically with the economy and avoids arbitrary absolute salary bounds.

## 46.2 Contract Duration From the Population Age Curve

Contract duration should depend primarily on visible age and the **global configured population decline distribution**, not the player's hidden individual decline date.

Let:

```text
muDecline    = configured mean decline age
sigmaDecline = configured decline-age standard deviation
Dmin         = 1 season
Dmax         = 4 seasons
Amid         = muDecline + 1
k            = 0.625 × sigmaDecline
```

Calculate the continuous expected duration:

```text
Dmean = Dmin
      + (Dmax - Dmin)
        / (1 + exp((age - Amid) / k))
```

For the currently discussed population configuration around `muDecline = 30` and `sigmaDecline = 4`, this produces approximately:

```text
Age 18 → 3.98 seasons
Age 24 → 3.83
Age 27 → 3.50
Age 30 → 2.80
Age 32 → 2.20
Age 34 → 1.69
Age 36 → 1.36
Age 38 → 1.17
```

Do not hard-round `Dmean`, because that would create sharp exploitable age thresholds. Use deterministic stochastic rounding.

If:

```text
Dmean = floor(Dmean) + f
```

then assign:

```text
ceil(Dmean)  with probability f
floor(Dmean) otherwise
```

using a deterministic listing seed.

Example:

```text
Dmean = 2.8
→ 80% of equivalent seeded cases receive 3 seasons
→ 20% receive 2 seasons
```

Clamp the final duration to `1..4` seasons.

This produces a smooth age-related contract market without revealing or querying hidden individual development information.

The new free-agent path must not reuse the legacy `freeAgentSigningBonus()` direct-signing logic.

# 48. Recent Trade Price → Opening-Price Base

The seller's opening-price range (§64.1) is normally based on:

```ts
baseValue = player.value;
```

But a recently acquired player should initially be based on what the market
actually paid for him.

Conceptually:

```ts
gamesSinceTrade =
    currentRound - acquisitionRound;

t =
    clamp(
        gamesSinceTrade /
        RECENT_TRADE_FADE_OVER_GAMES,
        0,
        1
    );

baseValue =
    lerp(
        recentTradePrice,
        player.value,
        t
    );
```

Immediately after acquisition:

```text
t ≈ 0

base ≈ recent actual trade price
```

Later, after the fade window:

```text
t → 1

base → player.value
```

The seller's allowed opening-price range is then `[0.60 × base, 1.00 × base]`
(§64.1). `RECENT_TRADE_FADE_OVER_GAMES` counts league rounds played by the
owner since the acquisition, not wall-clock days (§51).

## 64.1 Seller-Defined Opening Price

The selling club may choose the auction's opening asking price.

However, the price must remain within a configurable range based on the player's **opening-price base value** `V`.

The base value `V` is:

```text
recent trade value, if the player was recently traded
     blended linearly toward player.value over the fade window
otherwise player.value
```

Specifically, let `p0` be the price of the player's most recent permanent
acquisition (club-to-club or free-agent signing). Let `gamesSinceTrade` be the
number of league rounds the current owner has played since that acquisition,
and let `FADE_OVER_GAMES` be the configurable fade window. Then:

```text
if no recent permanent trade:
    V = player.value

else if gamesSinceTrade >= FADE_OVER_GAMES:
    V = player.value

else:
    t = gamesSinceTrade / FADE_OVER_GAMES        (0 <= t <= 1)
    V = lerp(p0, player.value, t) = p0 + (player.value - p0) * t
```

The recent trade price is the **player's own** last permanent trade price, not
a similar-player market average (§47/§72). The blend uses games played
(league rounds), not wall-clock time, so behavior is stable across season
cadence changes (§51). Both `p0` and `player.value` stay in the season they
were recorded; a trade that occurred in a prior season is still within the
fade window if fewer than `FADE_OVER_GAMES` rounds have been played since.

Initial parameters:

```text
MIN_AUCTION_OPENING_VALUE_RATIO = 0.60
MAX_AUCTION_OPENING_VALUE_RATIO = 1.00
RECENT_TRADE_FADE_OVER_GAMES    = configurable (initial: 6)
```

For a player with base value `V`:

```text
minimumOpeningPrice =
    V × MIN_AUCTION_OPENING_VALUE_RATIO

maximumOpeningPrice =
    V × MAX_AUCTION_OPENING_VALUE_RATIO
```

Therefore:

```text
minimumOpeningPrice
    <= sellerOpeningPrice
    <= maximumOpeningPrice
```

Example (no recent trade):

```text

Base V:       $5.8M

Allowed Asking Price:
$3.48M – $5.80M

Asking Price:
[ $4.50M ]

[ List for Auction ]
```

Example (recently traded cheaply, early in the fade window):

```text

Last trade price:    $0.6M  (free-agent signing)
Games since trade:   1 of 6

t = 1/6
V = 0.6 + (5.8 - 0.6) × (1/6) ≈ $1.47M

Allowed Asking Price:
≈ $0.88M – $1.47M
```

The ratios and the fade window must be configurable game settings and must not
be hard-coded.

## 64.2 Purpose of the Price Range

The minimum prevents clubs from intentionally transferring valuable players to another controlled account for negligible prices.

The maximum prevents sellers from creating absurd opening prices and provides a consistent starting point for auctions.

The seller is still free to choose where inside the permitted range the auction begins.

A club that wants a quick sale can start near the minimum.

A club that believes there will be strong demand can start closer to the base value.

The system works identically from the first day of a new game world: with no
completed-transfer history, the base is simply `player.value`. When a recent
trade exists, the base is anchored to that actual trade price and fades back
to `player.value` over the configured number of games (§64.1).

## 64.3 Bids Above the Asking Price

The seller-defined asking price is only the **opening bid**.

Once bidding begins, clubs may bid above it normally.

The maximum amount that another club may bid remains governed by the existing **club-to-club maximum-bid cap**.

Therefore the two mechanisms serve different purposes:

```text
Opening-price range
    → prevents artificially cheap or unreasonable listings

Maximum-bid cap
    → prevents artificially expensive purchases / money transfers
```

For example:

```text
Player Value:       $5.8M
Allowed Opening:    $3.48M – $5.80M
Seller Opens At:    $5.0M

Auction bids:
$5.0M
$5.4M
$6.1M
$6.8M
...
```

Bidding may continue above the player's calculated value until the applicable maximum-bid cap for that buyer/seller combination is reached.

This is the primary protection against a dummy account intentionally transferring excessive money to another club.

## 64.4 Backend Enforcement

When creating the auction, the backend must:

1. verify that the player belongs to the selling club;
2. verify that the player is part of the senior squad;
3. verify that the player is currently eligible for transfer;
4. calculate the player's current value;
5. compute the opening-price base `V` (§64.1) from the player's own recent
   trade history and the games-since-trade fade;
6. calculate the permitted opening-price range from `V`;
7. reject any submitted asking price outside that range;
8. create the auction using the submitted price as the opening bid.

The backend must independently calculate the base value and the permitted range.

Values supplied by the client for player value, base value, minimum price, maximum price, or maximum bid must never be treated as authoritative.

The note below reflects the intended default range:

```text
I specifically prefer 60%–100% for the opening price. The seller doesn't need
to open at 120–150% of value because competitive bidding can already take the
price there. The upper auction limit belongs in the maximum-bid-cap rule, not
in the seller's asking-price rule.
```

When the seller does not supply an asking price, the system uses the base value
as the opening bid (i.e. 100% of `V`), keeping the listing path deterministic
and usable from a fresh game world with no history.

# 79. Multi-Account Abuse — Structural Protections

The system should assume multiple accounts cannot always be detected.

Therefore protect the economy through market rules.

### Protection 1 — no buyer selection

Seller cannot choose the destination club.

### Protection 2 — public market

Every permanent transfer is public.

### Protection 3 — system-generated floor

Seller cannot give away a $10M player for $1.

### Protection 4 — asymmetric club-transfer maximum

A disposable lower/equal-division club cannot massively overpay a stronger seller: its cap remains at the strict `150%` baseline. A stronger-division club buying from a weaker division receives a progressively looser cap derived from the division gap (§10/§102.13.3), with diminishing returns and exactly `300%` at the largest gap.

### Protection 5 — proxy clearing price

A single account entering an absurd maximum does not automatically transfer that full amount.

### Protection 6 — maximum funds reserved

Disposable clubs cannot make unsupported simultaneous commitments.

### Protection 7 — financial capacity validation

Clubs cannot simply ignore unavoidable payroll commitments.

### Protection 8 — hidden bidders

Coordination is less convenient.

### Protection 9 — irreversible bids

Shill bids carry real risk.

### Protection 10 — club-to-club transfer cooldown

The same player cannot be ping-ponged repeatedly to move money.

### Protection 11 — free-agent recent-trade opening-price base

Cheap system acquisition does not automatically create a high full-value opening price: the seller's opening range is based on the low recent trade price and fades to `player.value` over games played (§48/§64.1).

### Protection 12 — public loan exposure

A lender cannot privately time an FCFS listing for a second account.

## 102.1 Current Implementation Baseline

The current game is not a transfer service backed directly by independent
market tables. It is a single global in-memory `World` that is rebuilt from
Prisma and persisted as a full normalized snapshot. All authenticated game
mutations currently use `withGlobalLock()` plus `Save.revision` optimistic
concurrency. Worker jobs use the same pattern through `runJob()`.

The implementation currently has these market states:

* `World.auctions` contains legacy auction listings; `Auction` and `AuctionBid`
  persist them.
* `World.loans` contains immediate, claimable-or-recalled loan records; `Loan`
  persists them.
* Free agents are only players with `clubId === null`; there is no free-agent
  listing, deadline, relist, or bid persistence.
* `Player.onSale` and `Player.salePrice` are duplicated listing state. The fixed
  price path is still exposed by the API and frontend.
* `transferPlayer()` is exported and is called by routes, AI, and legacy tests,
  so it is currently a direct movement bypass.
* `auctionProcessor` runs frequently, but `daily.ts`, `world.ts`, and rollover
  code still contain additional legacy auction/AI paths.

The first implementation decision is therefore architectural: either implement
the new services as the only domain boundary over the existing global `World`
and snapshot persistence, or migrate all market operations to authoritative
relational transactions. Do not mix an in-memory authoritative bid state with
independent relational settlement state. The smaller repository-compatible
option is to keep the global lock/revision boundary, add the new state to
`World`, and persist the complete market mutation in the existing Prisma
transaction.

## 102.2 Product Decisions Captured

The following decisions are now part of this plan:

* Free-agent listings generate fixed salary and contract-duration terms at
  listing creation. They do not snapshot the old terms and do not use
  `freeAgentSigningBonus()`.
* An unclaimed loan remains open after it becomes claimable until it is claimed,
  the player's contract ends, or the lender cancels/revokes it.
* Lender cancellation/revocation is allowed only before a successful claim;
  a completed loan cannot be recalled.
* `Player.potential` is hidden from human users. It must be removed from
  human-facing snapshots/API types and excluded from AI market inputs, together
  with `developmentProfile`.
* Only the global multiplayer world is supported. There is no separate legacy
  single-player transfer behavior or compatibility market.
* Both board confidence and fan confidence are removed, including persistence,
  API types, UI, match updates, payroll consequences, warnings, and tests.
* The club-to-club maximum-value ceiling is bidder-specific and derived from the
  **division gap** (§10/§102.13.3). It uses a strict `150%` baseline when the
  buyer is in the same or a weaker division than the seller, then relaxes
  smoothly according to the normalized division gap with diminishing returns,
  reaching exactly `300%` at the largest possible gap. It is independent of the
  budget curve. Free-agent bids are limited by the shared safe-budget validator
  rather than by `player.value`.
* During an active auction the selling club is public, all bidder identities are
  private, and only the winning club becomes public after settlement.
* Youth/academy players are not eligible for auction, free-agent, or loan markets
  until promoted to the senior squad.
* Loan cancellation is pre-claim only; there is no post-claim recall.
* Club-to-club auctions last 24 hours, have a bounded soft close, and may not cross
  season rollover; free-agent listings may cross rollover.

## 102.10 Finance and AI Boundary

`MarketFinanceService` uses the deterministic safe-budget model defined in section 24. It is no longer an open balancing decision.

For each proposed market commitment, compute the minimum projected cash balance over the funded horizon and reject any maximum above `SafeMarketBudget`.

Required inputs include:

* current cash and negative cash;
* active maximum reservations;
* accrued and future payroll for existing players;
* inherited auction salary, generated free-agent salary, and loan salary;
* stadium and other already committed expenses;
* guaranteed income at 100%;
* uncertain recurring income at a conservative P10 estimate, shrunk toward the
  division distribution with `w = n / (n + 8)`;
* a liquidity reserve of at least one full payroll cycle, or the P90 one-cycle
  forecast error if that estimate is larger and enough history exists.

The forecast horizon runs through the end of the furthest season whose budget allocation has already been unlocked for the club. If next-season allocation becomes available before the season begins, that allocation may count immediately, but next-season player payroll does not begin until the club actually enters that season.

The backend applies this validation to human bids, AI bids, free-agent signings, and loan claims. AI safe budgets must call the same validator rather than reproducing a looser calculation. Existing AI contract renewals should also use the same affordability boundary instead of `club.cash > demand`.

The AI strategy must receive a public-player projection containing current football data, contract/salary data, squad context, and public listing state. It must not receive full `Player`, `MarketBid`, or `World` objects if those expose hidden potential, development profile, or competing maximums. Deterministic noise and one-time evaluation state must use persisted identifiers, not the shared gameplay RNG or a re-evaluation loop.

## 102.13 Resolved Core-Market Product Decisions

### 102.13.1 Generated Free-Agent Salary and Contract Duration

Generate free-agent salary from the live contracted-player salary market using the robust log-value comparable model defined in section 46:

```text
di = ln(Vi / Vp)
wi = exp(-(di²) / (2 × ln(2)²))
marketSalary = exp(weightedMedian(ln(Si), wi))
```

Widen the kernel when effective sample size is below 25, add deterministic `exp(0.05 × Z)` negotiation noise with `Z` clamped to ±1.5, then clamp to the comparable weighted 5th-95th salary-percentile band.

Generate contract duration from visible age and the configured population decline-age distribution:

```text
Amid = muDecline + 1
k = 0.625 × sigmaDecline

Dmean = 1 + 3 / (1 + exp((age - Amid) / k))
```

Use deterministic stochastic rounding and clamp to 1-4 seasons. Never use the player's hidden individual decline date, hidden potential, or `developmentProfile`.

### 102.13.2 Safe-Budget Forecast

Use the section-24 `SafeMarketBudget` formula. Forecast to the end of the furthest season whose budget allocation has already been unlocked.

```text
SafeMarketBudget =
    max(0,
        min_t(
            currentCash
          - activeReservations
          + safeExpectedIncome(t)
          - committedExpenses(t)
          - acquisitionPayroll(t)
        )
        - minimumLiquidityReserve
    )
```

Rules:

* guaranteed income counts at 100%;
* uncertain recurring income uses P10 with club-to-division shrinkage
  `w = n / (n + 8)`;
* speculative sale/prize/promotion income does not count;
* reserve is at least one full payroll cycle, or P90 one-cycle forecast error if
  larger and statistically supportable;
* if next-season allocation is unlocked early, it may count immediately, but
  next-season salary obligations begin only when that season actually starts.

Humans and AI use the same validator.

### 102.13.3 Maximum-Bid Cap Scope and Division-Gap Curve

The value-based maximum bid cap applies **only to club-to-club auctions**, but it is not a universal `150%` ceiling.

The cap is asymmetric according to the **division gap between buyer and seller**, rather than their configured division budgets.

This keeps transfer-market balancing independent from the budget-distribution curve. Changes to division budgets must not implicitly change transfer-market caps.

Assume:

```text
Division 1 = strongest division
Division 2 = next strongest
Division 3 = weaker
...
```

Let:

```text
Dbuyer  = buyer's division number
Dseller = seller's division number
Dmax    = total number of divisions
```

Calculate the favorable division gap:

```text
favorableGap =
    max(0, Dseller - Dbuyer)
```

Therefore:

```text
same division
→ favorableGap = 0

higher-division club buying from lower division
→ favorableGap > 0

lower-division club buying from higher division
→ favorableGap = 0
```

Normalize the gap across the current pyramid depth:

```text
normalizedGap =
    favorableGap
    / max(1, Dmax - 1)
```

Initial configuration:

```text
BASE_CLUB_TRANSFER_CAP = 1.50
MAX_CLUB_TRANSFER_CAP_BONUS = 1.50
CLUB_TRANSFER_CAP_CURVE_K = 2.0
```

Calculate the normalized diminishing-return curve:

```text
curve =
    (1 - exp(-CLUB_TRANSFER_CAP_CURVE_K × normalizedGap))
    /
    (1 - exp(-CLUB_TRANSFER_CAP_CURVE_K))
```

Then:

```text
capMultiplier =
    BASE_CLUB_TRANSFER_CAP
    + MAX_CLUB_TRANSFER_CAP_BONUS × curve
```

The bidder-specific maximum becomes:

```text
Club-to-club max = min(
    player.value × capMultiplier,
    SafeMarketBudget
)
```

Properties:

* same-division transfer: exactly `150%`;
* a lower-division club buying from the same or a stronger division: still exactly `150%`;
* a stronger-division club buying from a weaker division: progressively looser cap;
* the largest possible division gap reaches exactly `300%`;
* diminishing returns make the first few division differences matter more than later ones;
* the result automatically adapts if the number of divisions changes;
* division numbers are used directly rather than configured budgets, keeping the transfer-cap system independent from the separate budget curve.

With the initial parameters, the curve behaves approximately as follows:

```text
Relative pyramid gap        Approx. cap

0%                          150%
10%                         181%
20%                         207%
30%                         229%
40%                         247%
50%                         262%
60%                         274%
70%                         284%
80%                         291%
90%                         296%
100%                        300%
```

For example, in a 10-division pyramid:

```text
D2 buys from D2
→ gap = 0
→ cap = 150%

D2 buys from D3
→ gap = 1 / 9
→ cap ≈ 184%

D2 buys from D5
→ gap = 3 / 9
→ cap ≈ 235%

D1 buys from D10
→ gap = 9 / 9
→ cap = 300%

D5 buys from D1
→ favorableGap = 0
→ cap = 150%
```

This deliberately protects the abuse-prone direction most strongly.

A disposable lower-division club cannot use an extreme overpayment to funnel money into a stronger main club:

```text
lower division
→ buys from higher division
→ 150% cap
```

Conversely, a stronger club may legitimately pay a larger premium when purchasing talent from a weaker division:

```text
higher division
→ buys from lower division
→ progressively relaxed cap
```

The cap is calculated **per bidder**, because different bidding clubs may belong to different divisions.

Therefore the auction itself must not store one universal:

```text
maximumAllowedBid
```

Instead, the service calculates the allowed ceiling whenever a club submits or increases its maximum bid.

For audit purposes, each accepted maximum-bid change should record the inputs used at that moment:

```text
buyerDivision
sellerDivision
totalDivisions
favorableGap
normalizedGap
capMultiplier
maximumAllowedAtSubmission
```

Free-agent bids have **no player-value multiplier cap**:

```text
Free-agent max = SafeMarketBudget
```

Free-agent signing money is paid to the system, so an above-value winning bid is a legitimate competitive money sink rather than a transfer of money between clubs.

AI remains constrained by:

```text
its own rational valuation
SafeMarketBudget
the same bidder-specific club-transfer cap used by humans
```

No AI-specific cap logic may bypass or reproduce this calculation separately.

### 102.13.4 Market Identity Privacy

During an active club-to-club auction:

```text
PUBLIC
- selling club
- current price
- bidder count
- deadline

PRIVATE
- current leading club
- all competing bidder identities
- all losing bidder identities
- every competing maximum bid
```

The authenticated bidder may see only its own maximum and whether it is currently leading.

After settlement:

```text
PUBLIC
- selling club
- winning club
- final price

STILL PRIVATE
- losing bidder identities
- all maximum bids other than the requesting club's own historical value if
  the product chooses to show that club its own history
```

### 102.13.5 Youth Market Eligibility

Youth/academy players are not eligible for auction. They also do not enter the senior free-agent or loan markets while they remain youth players.

```text
academy/youth player
→ develop / remain in academy
→ promote to senior squad
→ normal senior-market eligibility
```

Youth dismissal/release remains part of the academy lifecycle. Backend services must enforce this rule even if the UI already omits youth players.

### 102.13.6 Loan Cancellation

Lender cancellation is strictly **pre-claim only**.

```text
EXPOSURE / unclaimed
→ lender may cancel

CLAIMABLE / still unclaimed
→ lender may cancel

CLAIMED / active loan
→ lender cannot cancel
→ lender cannot recall
```

After claim, the loan ends only at the earliest configured terminal event, normally current-season end or contract expiry. The old recall API/UI and random early-ending behavior must be removed.

### 102.13.7 Auction Duration and Rollover

The new 24-hour auction duration replaces the legacy `auctionDurationDays = 7`.

```text
AUCTION_DURATION = 24 hours
SOFT_CLOSE_WINDOW = 5 minutes
SOFT_CLOSE_EXTENSION = 5 minutes
MAX_SOFT_CLOSE_EXTENSION = 30 minutes
```

Club-to-club auctions may not cross rollover:

```text
now + 24h + 30m <= seasonRolloverAt
```

must be true when the listing is created, and every extension remains capped at `seasonRolloverAt`.

Rollover policy:

```text
Club-to-club auction   must settle before rollover
Free-agent listing    may remain active across rollover
Unclaimed loan        cancel at rollover
Active loan           end and return player at rollover
```

If invalid legacy state leaves a club-to-club auction active at rollover, cancel it, release reservations, preserve an audit record, and do not transfer cash or player ownership.
