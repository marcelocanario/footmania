# Financial Cushion & Automatic Payroll Intervention — Implementation Plan

## 1. Objective

Replace the complex projected-solvency / `SafeMarketBudget` model with a simpler deterministic financial system based only on **known current commitments**.

The system must:

- allow human managers to make financially risky decisions;
- clearly warn them before and after they enter financial danger;
- prevent AI clubs from knowingly creating an unsafe financial position;
- preserve binding auction commitments;
- automatically intervene when a club remains unable to pay its obligations;
- recover distressed clubs through forced player departures rather than bankruptcy, manager firing, loans, points deductions, or financial relegation;
- prevent the intervention system from becoming an infinite-money exploit.

This plan supersedes the forecast-heavy financial validation currently described in the transfer overhaul, while retaining its market reservations, proxy bidding, auction floors, free-agent market, anti-abuse rules, and atomic settlement architecture. 

The multiplayer rules concerning season allocations, provisional clubs, salary freezes, and global season timing remain unchanged. 

---

# 2. Core Financial Metric

Introduce one simple derived value:

```ts
FinancialCushion =
    club.cash
    - activeBidCommitments
    - remainingSalaryCommitments;
```

Where:

```text
club.cash
```

is the club's current actual cash balance.

```text
activeBidCommitments
```

is the sum of durable active market reservations for bids the club is currently committed to.

```text
remainingSalaryCommitments
```

is the known salary the club is expected to pay through the end of its funded/current season.

Do not include estimated future income.

---

# 3. Explicitly Do Not Forecast Income

Remove financial calculations based on:

```text
P10 income
income distributions
club/division income shrinkage
Monte Carlo simulation
forecast error distributions
future expected gate receipts
future expected prize money
possible transfer proceeds
promotion rewards
future unissued budgets
```

The financial model should be deliberately pessimistic and simple.

Future income helps the club only when the money actually arrives.

---

# 4. Current Commitments

For the first implementation:

```ts
CurrentCommitments =
    ActiveBidCommitments
    + RemainingSalaryCommitments;
```

Do not start adding speculative expenses.

Only add another commitment category later if it represents a genuinely binding and unavoidable scheduled payment.

---

# 5. Salary Commitment Calculation

Create one authoritative helper:

```ts
calculateRemainingSalaryCommitments(club, season)
```

It must reuse the game's actual payroll rules.

Include salary from the next unpaid payroll through season end for players whose salary the club is responsible for.

### Include

```text
Owned active players
Loaned-in players where borrower pays salary
Players covered by existing binding salary rules
```

### Exclude

```text
Loaned-out players
Provisional salary periods before activation
Players already permanently transferred away
```

The loan overhaul already defines the borrower as responsible for 100% of loan wages. 

---

# 6. Contingent Salary From Leading Market Bids

If the club is currently leading a binding acquisition auction, the player's known salary should also be included as a contingent salary commitment.

For a transfer auction:

```text
existing player salary
× remaining payroll periods after acquisition
```

For a free agent:

```text
generated listing salary
× remaining payroll periods after acquisition
```

If the club is outbid:

```text
bid reservation released
contingent salary commitment removed
```

This prevents the financial display from pretending that winning the player has no payroll effect.

---

# 7. Provisional Clubs

For an `ACTIVE` club:

```text
commitment season = current season
```

For a `PROVISIONAL` club:

```text
commitment season = upcoming season
```

because provisional clubs may already possess and spend their next-season allocation, while salaries begin only when that season activates. 

Therefore:

```text
provisional salaries before activation = 0
```

but financial warnings should include the salary commitments that will begin when the club becomes active.

---

# 8. Human Clubs May Have a Negative Financial Cushion

Humans are allowed to take risks.

Example:

```text
Cash                         $12M
Bid commitments               $3M
Remaining salaries           $11M
--------------------------------
Financial cushion            -$2M
```

This does **not** automatically block the action.

Instead the game warns the user clearly.

---

# 9. Hard Immediate-Cash Rule Still Exists

Allowing financial irresponsibility does not mean allowing fictional money.

For a new immediate expense:

```ts
ImmediateAvailableCash =
    club.cash
    - activeBidCommitments;
```

A human may not voluntarily create a new immediate obligation exceeding that amount.

Example:

```text
Cash                         $10M
Reserved maximum bids         $7M
Immediate available cash      $3M
```

The club cannot spend:

```text
$5M on a stadium upgrade
```

because the $7M bid is binding.

But it may spend the $3M even if doing so leaves:

```text
FinancialCushion < 0
```

and therefore places future payroll at risk.

---

# 10. Human Auction Rules

Keep the existing club-to-club bidder-specific value cap.

For humans:

```ts
maximumAllowedBid = min(
    bidderSpecificPlayerValueCap,
    ImmediateAvailableCash
);
```

Do **not** additionally restrict the human to:

```text
FinancialCushion >= 0
```

The player may knowingly reserve money that would otherwise have been needed for future wages.

That is part of the financial gameplay.

---

# 11. Human Free-Agent Rules

Free-agent maximum:

```ts
maximumAllowedFreeAgentBid =
    ImmediateAvailableCash;
```

There remains no player-value cap for free agents, as already specified. 

The generated salary affects:

```text
FinancialCushion
```

and may trigger a warning.

It does not block the human bid solely because future payroll becomes unsafe.

---

# 12. Human Loans and Contracts

A human may:

```text
claim a loan
renew a contract
accept a higher salary
```

even if doing so causes:

```text
FinancialCushion < 0
```

provided any immediate payment can actually be funded.

The user must receive a warning before confirming such an action.

---

# 13. AI Financial Rule

AI clubs must obey:

```ts
FinancialCushionAfterDecision >= 0;
```

Equivalent:

```ts
cashAfterDecision >=
    bidCommitmentsAfterDecision
    + remainingSalaryCommitmentsAfterDecision;
```

This is the AI's hard financial safety rule.

---

# 14. AI Must Use the Same Commitment Calculator

Do not implement separate affordability formulas inside:

```text
AI transfer logic
AI contract logic
AI loans
AI stadium logic
```

Instead:

```ts
FinancialService.getFinancialCushion(club)

FinancialService.evaluateAIDecision(club, decision)
```

The AI strategy decides whether something is desirable.

The financial service decides whether it is affordable.

---

# 15. AI Acquisition Example

Suppose:

```text
Cash                         $20M
Current bids                  $2M
Remaining salaries           $12M

Current cushion               $6M
```

AI considers:

```text
New maximum bid               $5M
Player remaining salary       $2M
```

Result:

```text
$20M - $7M bids - $14M salaries = -$1M
```

Reject.

The AI may reduce its calculated maximum if appropriate.

# 16. AI Financial Pressure

If an AI becomes unsafe for reasons outside its voluntary decisions:

```text
FinancialCushion < 0
```

then:

```text
stop discretionary purchases
stop expensive acquisitions
increase financialPressure in AI sell scoring
prefer reducing payroll
```

The AI should attempt to recover through the normal public marketplace.

The existing transfer-market `financialPressure` selling factor can use `FinancialCushion` directly.

# 17. Human Warning States

Use three simple financial states.

## SAFE

```ts
FinancialCushion >= 0
```

Example:

```text
Financial cushion: $4.2M
```

No special warning.

---

## AT RISK

```ts
FinancialCushion < 0
&& club.cash >= 0
```

Show:

```text
Financial Warning

Your current cash does not cover your existing bids and
remaining salary commitments through season end.

Financial cushion: -$2.1M

Future income may improve this position, but if your cash
balance becomes negative and remains negative until a later
payroll cycle, players may be forced to leave the club.
```

---

## NEGATIVE CASH

```ts
club.cash < 0
```

Show a stronger persistent warning:

```text
Financial Emergency

Current cash: -$1.7M

If the club is still in a negative cash position when the
next payroll cycle is processed, a financial intervention
may force players to leave.
```

Display:

```text
Next payroll: <timestamp>
```

# 18. Warn Before Risky Actions

When a human action changes:

```text
FinancialCushion >= 0
```

to:

```text
FinancialCushion < 0
```

show a confirmation warning.

Example:

```text
This bid would reduce your financial cushion from:

+$1.8M
to
-$2.4M

Your current cash would no longer be sufficient to cover
your known bids and salaries through season end.

Continue?
```

The backend remains authoritative.

---

# 19. One-Payroll-Cycle Grace Period

Do not liquidate players immediately when cash first becomes negative.

At every payroll:

```ts
const wasNegativeBeforePayroll = club.cash < 0;

settlePayroll(club);

if (
    wasNegativeBeforePayroll &&
    club.cash < 0
) {
    runFinancialIntervention(club);
}
```

Therefore:

### First failed payroll

```text
cash was positive
→ payroll makes cash negative
→ warning only
```

The club has until the next payroll to recover.

### Next payroll

If the club is still negative:

```text
financial intervention
```

If income or player sales restored:

```text
cash >= 0
```

before the next payroll:

```text
no intervention
```

No separate persisted confidence/default counter is necessary.

---

# 20. Negative Cash From Other Events

A previously valid binding commitment may later push cash negative.

Example:

```text
auction settlement
```

If this occurs between payroll cycles:

```text
show NEGATIVE CASH warning immediately
```

but financial intervention still occurs only when the next payroll cycle reaches the trigger condition.

---

# 21. Financial Intervention Goal

The intervention does **not** merely restore:

```text
cash = 0
```

It must attempt to leave the club able to cover all remaining known commitments through season end.

Target:

```ts
TargetCash =
    activeBidCommitments
    + remainingSalaryCommitmentsAfterIntervention;
```

The intervention should attempt to achieve:

```ts
club.cash >= TargetCash;
```

This is important because otherwise the generated replacement players could cause another automatic intervention immediately afterward.

---

# 22. Replacement Salaries Must Be Included Before Liquidation

Replacement players must not be persisted after the system decides it is financially safe.

Instead:

1. determine which players may leave;
2. generate replacement-player previews in memory;
3. calculate their salary commitments;
4. include those salaries in intervention calculations;
5. execute liquidation;
6. only then persist the replacement players.

This makes the intervention mathematically self-contained.

---

# 23. Process Existing Outgoing Auctions First

When financial intervention begins, inspect every active transfer auction belonging to the distressed club.

This occurs before system liquidation.

---

# 24. Auction With No Bids

If:

```text
bidderCount = 0
```

then:

```text
cancel auction immediately
```

The player returns to the intervention candidate pool.

No cancellation penalty.

The club is being financially restructured.

---

# 25. Auction With Existing Valid Bids

If an outgoing auction already has a valid leader:

```text
settle the auction immediately
```

The club must accept the offer already on the table.

Use:

```text
current proxy clearing price
```

not:

```text
leading bidder's private maximum
```

Example:

```text
Leader maximum:        $12M
Current auction price: $9.4M

Forced settlement:
$9.4M
```

This preserves the eBay-style proxy rules. 

---

# 26. Forced Auction Settlement

Forced settlement must reuse normal auction settlement logic.

It should:

```text
determine valid leader
charge current clearing price
credit distressed seller
transfer player
release losing reservations
release winning excess reservation
record market history
publish normal transaction result
```

The only difference is:

```text
deadline is overridden by financial intervention
```

Do not create a second transfer implementation.

---

# 27. Recalculate After Existing Auction Settlements

After all applicable outgoing auction settlements/cancellations:

```ts
recalculate club.cash
recalculate activeBidCommitments
recalculate remainingSalaryCommitments
```

Some clubs may now be financially safe.

If:

```ts
FinancialCushion >= 0
```

after accounting for replacement salaries:

```text
no system player liquidation required
```

---

# 28. Loaned Players Are Excluded

Financial intervention must never system-liquidate:

```text
loaned-out players
loaned-in players
```

### Loaned in

The club does not own the player.

### Loaned out

The player remains with the borrower until the normal loan termination event.

Do not destroy another club's squad because the lender became insolvent.

---

# 29. Unclaimed Loan Listings

An unclaimed outgoing loan listing has no cash value.

If its player is required as a financial-intervention candidate:

```text
cancel the unclaimed loan listing
```

then treat the player as normally owned and available for liquidation.

Claimed/active loans remain excluded.

---

# 30. System Liquidation Candidate Pool

After auction processing, eligible forced-sale candidates are:

```text
senior player
owned by distressed club
not loaned in
not loaned out
not already transferred
not youth/academy
not otherwise unavailable
```

Financial intervention overrides normal voluntary transfer cooldowns.

A recently purchased player cannot make himself immune from unpaid-wage intervention merely because voluntary resale is normally blocked.

---

# 31. Prevent Intervention-Generated Infinite Money

Players generated as replacements by financial intervention must carry:

```ts
financialInterventionGeneratedSeasonId
```

For the remainder of that season they are:

```text
NOT eligible for another system-funded financial liquidation
```

They may still:

```text
play normally
develop normally
be sold through a real public auction if otherwise eligible
```

Real-market money is allowed.

Repeated system creation → system liquidation → creation loops are not.

At the next season:

```text
normal intervention eligibility resumes
```

---

# 32. System Liquidation Value

For every eligible player:

```ts
LiquidationPrice =
    getNormalAuctionOpeningPrice(player);
```

Use the same helper as voluntary auction creation.

Therefore it automatically respects:

```text
normal auction floor
recent-market-price anchor
existing value rules
```

Do not hardcode:

```ts
player.value * 0.85
```

inside the financial system.

The transfer-market service remains the authoritative source of minimum listing price. 

---

# 33. Player Goes to the System

A system-liquidated player:

```text
leaves the club
club receives LiquidationPrice
player becomes a free agent
```

The club keeps any surplus generated by the selected liquidation set.

Example:

```text
Required recovery: $10.0M

Selected players:
$6.0M
$4.5M

Recovered:
$10.5M

Surplus retained:
$0.5M
```

Do not confiscate the difference.

---

# 34. System-Liquidated Player Becomes a Normal Free Agent

After liquidation:

```text

```

Create a normal free-agent listing using the existing FA system.

That listing should use:

```text
normal FA opening-price rules
normal generated salary
normal generated contract duration
normal proxy bidding
normal deadline
```

The transfer overhaul already specifies that free agents use their own competitive market and that signing money goes to the system.

# 35. Former Club Cannot Immediately Re-Sign the Player

The resulting free-agent listing must store:

```ts
blockedClubId = formerClubId;
```

That club cannot bid on that specific free-agent listing.

This prevents:

```text

→ immediately signs player back
```

---

# 36. Repurchase Restriction Ends After Another Club Signs Him

Once another club signs the player:

```text
the special restriction is finished
```

If that player later appears in an ordinary club-to-club auction:

```text
former club may bid normally
```

There is no lifetime ban.

---

# 37. Replacement Player Generation

For every player permanently lost due to intervention:

```text
generate exactly one replacement
of the same position
```

This applies to:

```text
system liquidations
forced early auction settlements
```

The goal is to maintain a valid roster.

---

# 38. Replacement Quality

Do **not** create special weak "emergency players."

Replacement players must be generated using the same distribution used when generating players for a **new club entering the distressed club's current division/tier**.

Therefore replacements naturally include variation such as:

```text
possible starter-quality player
average squad player
bench-quality player
```

The replacement is random according to the normal new-club player-generation model.

---

# 39. Do Not Invent a New Overall Formula

The supplied multiplayer/transfer plans do not fully document the actual algorithm that determines a newly generated player's overall. They establish that clubs receive generated rosters and that existing club/player-generation logic exists, but they do not specify the complete distribution.  

Therefore the coding agent must first inspect the existing implementation used for:

```text
new human club roster generation
AI/filler club roster generation where appropriate
Club.level / tier-driven player generation
```

Then reuse or extract that logic.

Do not invent:

```text
special financial-intervention overall
fixed OVR penalty

bench replacement formula
```

# 40. Preserve the Existing Distribution

If new-club generation currently works through a full-roster distribution rather than a simple per-player function, refactor it so intervention replacements can sample from the **same underlying distribution**.

The intervention generator should effectively answer:

> If a new club entering this division generated another player at this position, what could that player look like?

It must preserve the same randomness.

# 41. Deterministic Randomness for Retry Safety

Generation should remain random from the player's perspective, but use deterministic seeded randomness for each intervention.

Example seed:

```text
financialInterventionId
+
departedPlayerId
+
position
```

Therefore:

```text
same intervention retry
→ same replacement
```

A server crash must not reroll a replacement until a stronger player appears.

# 43. Financial Recovery Mathematics

After forced auction settlements, define:

```text
C = current cash

B = active remaining bid commitments

S = remaining salary commitments of current players

R = remaining salary commitments of replacements already
    required by forced auction settlements
```

Required recovery:

```ts
D = max(
    0,
    B + S + R - C
);
```

If:

```text
D = 0
```

no system liquidation is required.

---

# 44. Effective Recovery Value of a Candidate

For candidate player `i`:

```text
Pi = system liquidation price

Wi = remaining salary commitment removed
     when player i leaves

Gi = remaining salary commitment of
     player i's preview replacement
```

Define:

```ts
EffectiveRecovery_i =
    Pi
    + Wi
    - Gi;
```

This is the player's true contribution to fixing the club's finances.

A high-salary player therefore helps not only through his sale price, but also through salary relief.

---

# 45. Find the Best Combination of Players

Do not simply sell:

```text
highest-paid first
highest-value first
oldest first
```

Instead find a subset `S` satisfying:

```ts
sum(EffectiveRecovery_i for i in S) >= D;
```

while minimizing:

```ts
sum(EffectiveRecovery_i for i in S) - D;
```

In plain language:

> Sell the combination of players that solves the financial shortfall while producing the smallest possible surplus.

---

# 46. Subset Tie-Breaking

If multiple combinations produce the same smallest overshoot:

```text
1. choose the combination selling fewer players;
2. use deterministic player ordering/ID as final fallback.
```

This avoids unnecessarily dismantling the squad.

---

# 47. Exact Search Is Practical

Senior squads are small enough that this should use an exact subset-selection algorithm rather than a rough greedy rule.

Recommended:

```text
meet-in-the-middle subset search
```

For approximately 20–30 candidates this is cheap because complexity is approximately:

```text
O(2^(N/2))
```

rather than:

```text
O(2^N)
```

This financial intervention is also rare, so it does not need minute-ticker performance.

---

# 48. Never Select a Player Whose Sale Makes Recovery Worse

If:

```ts
EffectiveRecovery_i <= 0
```

do not system-liquidate that player merely for financial recovery.

Such a sale would not improve solvency once replacement wages are considered.

---

# 49. Insufficient Eligible Assets

It is theoretically possible that:

```text
all eligible system-liquidatable players
```

still cannot produce enough effective recovery.

In that case:

1. liquidate the best available positive-recovery set;
2. generate required replacements;
3. do not invent additional cash;
4. leave the club in financial distress;
5. preserve the negative-cash warning;
6. allow future real income or real market sales to recover the club.

Do not recursively sell newly generated players to the system.

Log this condition for monitoring because it should be rare.

---

# 50. Intervention Execution Order

The complete intervention should be:

```text
1. Acquire club/world financial intervention lock.

2. Re-read current authoritative state.

3. Resolve/cancel outgoing auctions:
   - no bid → cancel
   - valid bid → immediate normal settlement

4. Recalculate cash and commitments.

5. Determine forced-auction replacement requirements.

6. Preview their replacement players.

7. Calculate remaining financial recovery requirement.

8. Build eligible system-liquidation candidate pool.

9. Preview one same-position replacement per candidate.

10. Calculate EffectiveRecovery for each candidate.

11. Solve subset-selection problem.

12. System-liquidate selected players.

13. Create resulting free-agent listings.

14. Persist the already-previewed replacement players.

15. Recalculate final FinancialCushion.

16. Record intervention audit event.

17. Publish news/notifications.

18. Commit atomically.
```

# 79. Final Invariants

After implementation, the engine must guarantee:

```text
1. FinancialCushion = cash - binding bids - known remaining salaries.

2. No uncertain future income enters the calculation.

3. Humans may voluntarily make FinancialCushion negative.

4. Humans receive clear warnings before and after doing so.

5. Immediate expenses still require actual unreserved cash.

6. AI voluntary decisions may never make FinancialCushion negative.

7. Human and AI clubs use the same underlying commitment calculator.

8. Bid reservations remain binding.

9. First negative payroll produces a grace period.

10. A club still negative at the following payroll enters financial intervention.

11. Financial intervention attempts to fund all known commitments through season end.

12. Existing outgoing auctions with bids settle immediately at the current
    proxy clearing price.

13. Existing outgoing auctions without bids are cancelled.

14. Loaned-in and loaned-out players cannot be system-liquidated.

15. The system chooses a combination of players minimizing recovery overshoot.

16. Salary savings and replacement salary costs are included when choosing
    that combination.

17. System-liquidated players are paid at the normal minimum auction price.

18. The club keeps legitimate liquidation surplus.

19. System-liquidated players become normal free agents.

20. The former club cannot bid on that resulting FA listing.

21. The restriction ends once another club signs the player.

22. Every departing player receives one same-position generated replacement.

23. Replacement quality uses the same random generation distribution as a
    new club entering that division.

24. Replacement salaries are calculated before liquidation is finalized.

25. Financial-intervention replacements cannot themselves generate another
    system-funded liquidation during the same season.

26. Real public-market sales of those replacements remain legal.

27. Financial intervention never recursively generates players to manufacture
    system cash.

28. Every intervention is atomic, idempotent, auditable, and retry-safe.

29. No board confidence, overdraft penalty, manager firing, emergency financing,
    bankruptcy, points deduction, or financial relegation exists.

30. Player loss is the principal consequence of persistent financial
    mismanagement.
```
